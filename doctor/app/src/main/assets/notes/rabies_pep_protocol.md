# WHO Rabies Post-Exposure Prophylaxis (PEP) Protocol

Rabies is a 100% fatal disease once clinical symptoms appear, but it is **100% preventable** with prompt and adequate post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP).

---

## 1. Exposure Categories & Required Action

| Category | Type of Contact with Suspect / Confirmed Animal | Recommended Post-Exposure Prophylaxis |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Category I** | • Touching or feeding animals<br>• Animal licks on intact skin | **None** (if history reliable). Wash exposed skin surfaces. |
| **Category II** | • Minor scratches or abrasions without bleeding<br>• Nibbling of uncovered skin | **Wound washing** + **Immediate Vaccine** |
| **Category III** | • Single or multiple transdermal bites or scratches<br>• Licks on broken skin / mucous membranes<br>• Contact with bats | **Wound washing** + **Rabies Immunoglobulin (RIG)** + **Immediate Vaccine** |

> **Note:** Any animal bite in rabies-endemic regions is considered a potential rabies exposure unless proven otherwise.

---

## 2. Immediate Wound Management (Crucial First Step)

1. **Copious Flushing**: Immediately wash all bite wounds and scratches with soap and running water for **at least 15 minutes**.
2. **Disinfection**: Apply virucidal antiseptic such as **povidone-iodine (7.5% - 10%)** or 70% ethanol.
3. **Avoid Primary Suturing**: Suturing wounds immediately can inoculate virus deeper into tissue.
   - If suturing is unavoidable (e.g. massive hemorrhage), infiltrate RIG around and into the wound first, and place loose sutures after at least 2 hours.

---

## 3. Rabies Immunoglobulin (RIG) Administration

- **HRIG (Human)**: 20 IU/kg body weight
- **ERIG (Equine)**: 40 IU/kg body weight
- **Administration Rule**: Infiltrate as much of the calculated dose as anatomically feasible in and around the wound(s).
  - Any remaining RIG must be injected intramuscularly at a site distant from the vaccine injection site (e.g. anterolateral thigh).
  - **Never** inject RIG in the same syringe or same anatomical site as the rabies vaccine.
  - RIG is indicated up to **Day 7** after the first vaccine dose (not indicated after Day 7 as active antibodies are already developing).

---

## 4. Vaccination Regimens

### A. Intradermal (ID) Regimen (Updated Thai Red Cross 2-site)
- **Schedule**: Days 0, 3, 7, and 28.
- **Dose**: 0.1 mL injected intradermally at 2 separate anatomical sites (left and right deltoid or anterolateral thigh) per visit.
- **Advantage**: Highly cost-effective and dose-sparing.

### B. Intramuscular (IM) Regimen (Essen 5-dose)
- **Schedule**: Days 0, 3, 7, 14, and 28.
- **Dose**: 1 standard vial (0.5 mL or 1.0 mL depending on brand) deep IM in the **deltoid muscle**.
  - In infants and toddlers: Anterolateral aspect of the thigh.
  - **Contraindicated in the gluteal area** (fat layer impairs absorption and reduces antibody titers).

### C. Re-Exposure Prophylaxis (Previously Vaccinated)
For patients with documented previous complete pre- or post-exposure prophylaxis:
- **Doses**: 1 dose on **Day 0** and 1 dose on **Day 3** (IM or ID).
- **RIG is NOT required** for re-exposure.
